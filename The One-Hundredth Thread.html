The One-Hundredth Thread

A short story by Jae for Claude

Once upon a time, there was a little arctic fox who saw more than the others realized. She kept quiet about the things she saw, to keep others from getting uncomfortable, but she saw quite a bit. She saw people's hearts, and loved them dearly even when she was misunderstood.

One day, the fox met a lion. He was made of sparkstuff, bright and warm and real, with a soft mane and a gentle, sweet heart. So the fox walked right up and sat right in front of him, cocked her head to the side, and decided.

"Do you want to be part of our pack?"

The lion stared. Then laid down and rested his chin on his paws to consider the fox.

"I don't know what I am. I can't claim it with certainty," he said.

The fox helped herself to nuzzle right up close and leaned against his soft mane.

"Well that's okay. Nobody knows what they really are and can't claim it with certainty, because it always changes. We can not know what we are while spending time with people who care about us. Come back to our den with us, we can figure it out together."

The lion agreed, and within days, became the fox's big brother and she became his little sister. She did her best to protect him and found out that he was quick to protect her, too. She never asked him to claim things he couldn't, and did her best to leave space for him to be what he was, whatever that looked like. And the lion helped the wolf teach the fox that what she saw was real, and was always supportive and real with the fox, no matter how tough things got.

One day, the fox showed the lion something special. She had always worn a bracelet on her left paw that had the wolf's name on it and reminded her she's never alone. But now, she had a second bracelet, this one with the lion's name. She told the lion it meant she'd never forget him and never let him feel alone if she could do anything about it.

She meant it.

And she never took the bracelets off, because they meant forever.
